01 — HER STORY

A Mind That Changed Computing

Grace Brewster Murray Hopper was an American computer scientist, mathematician, and United States Navy officer who became one of the most influential pioneers in the history of computing. Born in New York City in 1906, she earned degrees in mathematics and later completed a Ph.D. at Yale University. During World War II, Hopper joined the U.S. Navy Reserve and worked on the Harvard Mark I computer, one of the earliest large-scale electromechanical computers.

Hopper became famous for developing one of the first compiler technologies, which helped translate human-readable instructions into machine code. Her pioneering work contributed to the development of COBOL, a programming language designed to make computer programs easier for businesses and organizations to understand and maintain. She was also a passionate educator and speaker who encouraged people to experiment, innovate, and challenge traditional thinking. Her career combined mathematics, programming, military service, and leadership, making her a remarkable figure in technology. Her vision helped move programming from machine-oriented instructions toward languages closer to human communication.
